Research Overview

People increasingly turn to search engines, social media, and AI chatbots to understand their health, and that information is often overwhelming, inconsistent, and hard to interpret. My research combines machine learning and multimedia (text, image, and video) processing with qualitative and quantitative methods to do two things in parallel: build tools that help users synthesize what their online health information exposure, and understand people: their attitudes, perceptions, and needs around online and AI-generated health information. Each strand informs the other, with the goal of supporting informed, equitable shared decision making.

Research Themes

Online Health Information & AI-Generated Content

What do patients encounter when they search online, and how well does AI-generated content serve them? I study the information people are exposed to, using breast reconstruction as a central case study, and explore how it reaches users and how tools can help them synthesize it.

Patient & Public Perspectives on AI in Healthcare

How do patients, survivors, and the public view AI in their care and the outcomes they may experience? I use mixed-methods studies and evidence synthesis to understand attitudes and perceptions that should shape how clinical AI is designed and communicated.

Engineering Education Research

Qualitative analysis is rich but labor-intensive. With the Center for Equity in Engineering at UT Austin, I explored how open-source large language models (e.g., Llama 3) can streamline and automate steps of qualitative data analysis pipelines, including a case study of students’ written reflections.

Undergraduate Work

Imaging & Machine Learning

As an undergraduate at UC Berkeley, I worked at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory’s Advanced Light Source. I primarily focused on using mixed-scale dense deep convolutional neural network to automate fiber center detection in micro-CT datasets of ceramic-matrix composites, a material used in gas turbines.
